Abstract

This study asks whether observable content and search signals can improve the prioritization of pages for human review. The work uses the pseudonymized FlyRank internship warehouse and constructs features from information available before a decision moment, with future clicks used as a directional decline target. A Random Forest ranking model was evaluated under client-grouped holdout validation, with leakage checks and explicit reason codes added before translating scores into actions. On the W07 audit slice, the grouped holdout produced ROC AUC 0.7223, average precision 0.5647, and Precision@50 0.5200; an earlier W05 random-split result of 0.9400 Precision@50 is treated as exploratory rather than as the headline because its split did not isolate clients. The resulting system is best interpreted as a bounded decision-support queue: it helps reviewers decide what to inspect first, but it does not prove that a page will decline or that a refresh will cause recovery.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams have finite review capacity. The operational question is therefore not “which page is bad?” but which page should a human inspect first? A useful ranking must favor candidates with enough evidence to matter while avoiding low-volume noise, seasonal movement, SERP-driven click loss, and other explanations that a simple score cannot distinguish.

The project uses Lane 2, Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring. The intended user is a content strategist, SEO analyst, or editor who needs a bounded review queue for refresh, expansion, protection, or monitoring. A false positive consumes scarce review capacity; a false negative can leave a potentially important decline unexamined.

Research question: Can a leakage-aware learned ranking improve the ordering of pages for human review compared with a simple prioritization rule?

3. Methodology

Task framing

The task is ranking/scoring. The model produces a probability-like decline score, and the operational output is an ordered queue. The primary decision metric is Precision@50 because the intended workflow has finite reviewer capacity.

Features

The W07 validated model uses word_count, search_volume, competition, cpc, historical seven-day average clicks, and historical seven-day summed engagement. Historical features are calculated from the period before the decision moment.

Label

The validated W07 target is a directional seven-day decline proxy:

future 7-day clicks < prior 7-day average clicks × 7

This is deliberately described as a proxy. It is not a business-value target, a recovery label, or evidence that a refresh would work.

Baseline and model

Earlier work established transparent refresh-priority rules using demand, freshness, position opportunity, and content context. The starter model comparison reported baseline and learned rankings on a 30,000-row slice. For the final audit, Random Forest was selected because it can represent non-linear interactions and provides a ranking score without requiring a brittle hand-written decision tree.

Validation

W07 uses GroupShuffleSplit by client_hash_id, with 30% of clients held out and random state 42. The validated holdout contains 7 clients and 1,135 rows. This is more conservative than the W05 random row split because the model is tested on clients it did not see during training.

Leakage checks

The project explicitly separates historical features from future targets. A Week-3 synthetic leakage experiment showed R² 0.7285 with an intentionally leaked same-day signal versus 0.6585 after removing it, demonstrating how easily apparent performance can be inflated. The final workflow also excludes upstream product decision outputs from the feature matrix.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The seven-day decline target is a proxy for future click movement, not a measure of business value or causal recovery.
  • The W07 holdout is limited to 7 unseen clients and 1,135 rows; it is evidence about this audit slice, not a production guarantee.
  • The W05 0.9400 Precision@50 result came from a row-level split and is therefore vulnerable to entity overlap; it is not the final validation claim.
  • The final W07 notebook does not contain a recomputed Week-4 operational baseline on the identical grouped holdout, so model-vs-baseline superiority on that split remains unestablished.
  • Low CTR can reflect SERP or AI-feature click loss rather than content weakness. Decline can also reflect consolidation, seasonality, or noise.
  • Content length thresholds are review heuristics, not universal requirements. Longer content can correlate with visibility without implying that every topic should be expanded.
  • The analysis is observational. It does not prove Google ranking factors, prove that a refresh causes recovery, or establish revenue lift.
Safe claim: The validated model produced a measurable ranking signal for this audit slice and can support human review prioritization. It should not be treated as an autonomous optimizer.

6. Ranked Recommendations

  1. Prioritize high-confidence, high-value candidates for senior review. Use high decline score plus the historical click × CPC proxy as prioritization context, not as booked revenue. Reason codes: HIGH_DECLINE_SCORE, HIGH_VALUE_AT_RISK.
  2. Review aging visible pages before treating age as a reason to rewrite. Pages at least 180 days old with meaningful historical clicks should be checked for freshness and continued intent fit. Reason code: AGING_VISIBLE.
  3. Assess thin visible pages for useful expansion, not word-count padding. A low word count should trigger a missing-subtopic review rather than an automatic rewrite. Reason code: THIN_VISIBLE.
  4. Separate low-evidence candidates into monitoring. Pages with weak historical click evidence should not consume the same editorial capacity as well-supported candidates. Reason code: LOW_EVIDENCE.
  5. Screen for competing explanations before editing. Check consolidation, seasonality, SERP/AI click loss, and noise before treating a decline score as a content problem.

These recommendations are intentionally human-in-the-loop. Publishing, deleting, rewriting, merging, or pruning content remains outside the automated decision boundary.
